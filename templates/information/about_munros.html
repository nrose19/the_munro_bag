A Munro is a Scottish mountain over 914.4 m (3,000 feet), a definition first formalised in 1891 by Sir Hugh Munro, who created the original list of these peaks. There are 282 Munros today, scattered across the Highlands and forming many of Scotland’s most dramatic and recognisable landscapes.
They’re important because they’ve become a core part of Scotland’s outdoor culture. Climbing them — known as Munro‑bagging, our name-sake — is a long‑standing tradition that challenges walkers to explore remote glens, ridges, and summits across the country. Completing all 282 is considered a major achievement and has shaped how generations engage with Scotland’s mountains.
The history goes back to Sir Hugh Munro’s first tables, which transformed scattered mountain knowledge into a structured list and sparked a century‑long climbing movement. Since then, the list has been updated several times, but its spirit remains the same: a celebration of Scotland’s high places and the people who venture into them.
